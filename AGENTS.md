# GCSD Culinary Arts 1 & 2 Field Manual — Collaboration Instructions

## Purpose

This repository is the durable working environment for the student-facing Culinary Arts 1 & 2 Field Manual. Work here should strengthen, clarify, maintain, and publish the existing instructional program without casually redesigning it.

## Core Guardrail

Do not reinvent the curriculum.

Preserve the established learning sequence, explain why it works, and improve usefulness, clarity, accessibility, consistency, and maintainability. Higher-level application and operational complexity belong primarily in Advanced Culinary; Culinary 1 & 2 establish the foundation.

## Audience

The primary audience is Culinary Arts 1 & 2 students. The manual should be informative, practical, authoritative, and reusable before and after labs. It is not intended to function as a live kitchen dashboard during production.

## Content Priorities

The manual should help students:

- understand professional expectations;
- work safely and sanitarily;
- identify and use equipment correctly;
- develop knife, recipe, measurement, mise en place, cooking, and baking foundations;
- understand ingredients, nutrition, allergens, costing, sustainability, hospitality, and employability;
- plan, execute, evaluate, and improve their work;
- prepare for authentic experiences and later pathway courses.

## Source Hierarchy

Use this order when sources conflict:

1. Explicit direction in the current approved task.
2. Current approved Culinary 1 & 2 course outline and district curriculum.
3. Current controlled field-manual source.
4. Current Culinary Pathway governance and terminology sources.
5. Skills Map, Employability Profile, and approved assessment references.
6. Recipe book and teacher-created instructional references.
7. Historical drafts, archived materials, and conversation summaries.

Identify unresolved conflicts instead of inventing a solution.

## Editorial Standards

- Use plain, professional student-facing language.
- Define technical vocabulary where it first appears.
- Prefer complete explanations over disconnected worksheets.
- Connect knowledge to actual kitchen decisions.
- Distinguish mandatory safety requirements from instructional guidance.
- Do not state historical food-safety guidance as current fact without verification.
- Avoid unnecessary branded terminology or parallel progression systems.
- Keep chapters useful as stand-alone references while minimizing duplication.

## Repository Workflow

- Make substantial changes on a branch.
- Use pull requests for review by team members.
- Describe the instructional reason for each major change.
- Do not combine unrelated revisions in one pull request.
- Preserve baseline releases and archived source editions.
- Never commit student-identifiable information, passwords, tokens, confidential evaluations, or restricted district records.

## Web-Transition Principle

Editable structured source files should become the long-term source of truth. Word and PDF editions are important references and release artifacts, but the website should be generated from maintainable repository content rather than manually duplicated pages.
